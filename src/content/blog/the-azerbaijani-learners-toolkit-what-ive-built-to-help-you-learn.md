## What I Have Been Building

Master Azerbaijani started with something personal.

I wanted to help my husband and children learn the language I grew up speaking. I wanted clear, practical resources for English speakers, especially families like mine. So I started building them.

This is the 48th article on this blog. I have spent most of those posts explaining the language and the culture around it. Today I want to do something different; show you everything I have built and how the pieces fit together.

Whether you are starting from zero, returning to a language you heard growing up, or trying to pass it on to your children, here is where each part fits.


## The App: A Path Through the Language

The Master Azerbaijani app gives you a sequence to follow. Vocabulary, pronunciation, practical phrases, and cultural context: built into lessons you work through at your own pace.

Ten modules are available now, with modules 11 through 15 coming in March 2027. Module 1 is free. Start there and get a feel for how the lessons work before committing to the rest.

The app works best as the center of your practice. Work through a lesson, say the examples out loud, and come back to anything that does not stick. Before moving on, try using something from the lesson yourself; introduce someone, ask a question, put a phrase into a message. That is how you find out what you actually know versus what you just recognized.

## Word of the Day: Small Pieces You Can Return To

For over a year, I have posted a new Azerbaijani word every day, pronunciation, meaning, and example phrases, in a short video format across [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/masterazerbaijani/), [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=61583629125594), [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@masterazerbaijani), and [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@MasterAzerbaijani).

That is more than a year of content already there, which means you have plenty to revisit beyond whatever today's word is.

Use whichever platform you already spend time on. When a word catches your attention, say it out loud, try a sentence of your own, and come back to it later to see what stayed. If it is useful to you, write it down with its example, not just the word alone.

The daily format is designed for exactly this: small, consistent exposure that builds over time without requiring a study session.


## The Blog: Explanations You Can Come Back To

Forty-eight posts now. Pronunciation, grammar, everyday expressions, history, culture, and the kind of context that does not fit inside a dictionary entry.

Use the blog alongside the app. When something in a lesson raises a question, or when you want to understand a pattern rather than just practice it, find the post that covers it.

A few places to start, depending on what you need:

- For the foundations: [Azerbaijani Alphabet Made Easy](/blog/azerbaijani-alphabet-made-easy) and [How to Ask Questions in Azerbaijani](/blog/how-to-ask-questions-in-azerbaijani)

After reading, pick one example and do something with it. Say it out loud, change a detail, try it in a conversation. Keep the post bookmarked for when the same question comes up again.


## The Printable Alphabet Bundle: A Reference You Can Keep Nearby

The Master Azerbaijani printable Alphabet Bundle includes a full alphabet poster, separate vowel and consonant posters, and individual letter posters with example words and pronunciation.

I made these because learners need something physical to refer to while they are reading and practicing, something they can glance at without breaking their flow.

Keep a poster near where you study. When a letter makes you hesitate, check the example, say the word out loud, and go back to the sentence you were reading.

For families learning together, these work well as a starting point. Pick a few letters, read the examples together, and look for the same letters in words your children already recognize.

## For Families and Heritage Learners

You may understand more Azerbaijani than you can comfortably say. Your children may recognize a few expressions from home but struggle to read them. Everyone needs a different starting point.

Take one word from a daily video and use it together. Ask a relative how they would put it in a sentence. Practice a greeting before a family call and then actually use it.

The resources work differently depending on where you are starting from. The app gives structure. The blog gives context. The daily videos give exposure. The alphabet materials give you something to refer back to when the letters slow you down.

Use what fits where you are right now.
