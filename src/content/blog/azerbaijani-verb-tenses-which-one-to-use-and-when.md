## Same Verb, Different Ending

You learn **gəlmək** - to come. Then you hear it with different endings:

- **Gəlirəm** - I am coming.
- **Gəldim** - I came.
- **Gələcəyəm** - I will come.

The verb still describes coming. The ending tells you how to place that action in time.

Those three are easy enough to separate. The questions begin when you meet **gəlmişəm**, or hear someone say **gələrəm** instead of **gələcəyəm**. Both **gələrəm** and **gələcəyəm** can be translated with "will." Both refer to the future. They are not the same.

To choose between them, you need to think about what you are actually telling the other person.

That is what this post is for. If you want to understand how those endings are built, the [suffixes post](/blog/azerbaijani-suffixes-the-building-blocks-of-fluency) covers the structure. This one is about when to use which form.


## The Present: Now and Every Day

The Azerbaijani present tense covers both something happening right now and something you do regularly. Same form, both uses.

> **İndi kitab oxuyuram.** - I am reading a book now.  
> **Hər axşam kitab oxuyuram.** - I read every evening.

**Oxuyuram** in both sentences. **İndi** tells you it is happening now. **Hər axşam** makes it a routine.

English splits these into two forms: "I am reading" and "I read." Azerbaijani uses one. Context does the separating.

The same applies to **alıram**. It can mean "I buy" as a habit and "I am buying" as something happening right now. Both are correct uses of the same form.

The present tense endings are **-ır / -ir / -ur / -ür**, following vowel harmony. You will see this in **yazıram**, **gəlirəm**, **oxuyuram**, **görürəm**.

Use this tense to talk about what you are doing right now, where you work, what you like, or what happens regularly.


## The Continuous Present: Almaqdayam

When you want to emphasize that something is actively in progress at this moment, Azerbaijani has a separate continuous form.

> **Alıram.** - I am buying / I buy.  
> **Almaqdayam.** - I am in the process of buying right now. *(more emphatic)*

**Alıram** already covers both present action and habit, as above. **Almaqdayam** makes the ongoing process explicit; you are stressing that it is happening right now, not just stating a fact.

You do not need **almaqdayam** every time something is happening in the present. But when you want to make the in-progress nature of the action clear, it is there. More on where English speakers tend to go wrong with this in the [grammar mistakes post](/blog/common-grammar-mistakes-english-speakers-make-in-azerbaijani).


## The Simple Past: What Happened

When you are telling someone what happened, the **-dı / -di / -du / -dü** past form is where you start.

> **Dünən anamla danışdım.** - I spoke to my mother yesterday.  
> **Evə gəldim, yemək yedim, sonra dincəldim.** - I came home, ate, and then rested.

This is the form you will use when recounting your day or telling events in sequence.

> **Aldım.** - I bought it.


## The -mış Past: Experience and Completed Actions

Now compare these two:

> **Dünən kitabı oxudum.** - I read the book yesterday. *(specific past event)*  
> **Bu kitabı oxumuşam.** - I have read this book. *(experience, relevant to now)*

The first recounts something that happened. The second tells someone that reading this book is part of your experience, and that experience matters to the current conversation.

This form is useful for:

- **Bakıda olmuşam.** - I have been to Baku.
- **Mən artıq yemişəm.** - I have already eaten.

That second sentence is exactly what you say when someone offers you food and you want to explain why you do not need a meal right now. You are not describing when you ate; you are explaining the current situation.

You will also hear the **-ıb / -ib / -ub / -üb** forms in this family:

> **Leyla artıq gəlib.** - Leyla has already arrived.

Keep these expressions together with the situations people use them in. The English translation is a guide, not a perfect match.


## The Future in the Past: Alacaqdım

This one surprises learners because it looks like a past tense but points toward the future, or rather, toward a future that was anticipated at some point in the past.

**Alacaqdım** means "I was going to buy it." It describes an intention or expectation that existed at an earlier moment.

> **Alacaqdım, amma pulum olmadı.** - I was going to buy it, but I did not have money.

In that sentence, **amma pulum olmadı** makes clear the purchase never happened. The verb form alone does not always establish that; context fills in whether the intention was followed through or not.

Compare it to **aldım**, I bought it, which simply states the action happened. In Azerbaijani, the form itself makes the distinction explicit between what you did and what you intended to do.


## The Definite Future: Alacağam / Alacam

The **-acaq / -əcək** form is the future tense for clear plans and firm intentions.

> **Sabah sənə zəng edəcəyəm.** - I will call you tomorrow.  
> **Alacağam.** - I will buy it. / I am going to buy it.

In everyday speech you will hear this contracted to **alacam**. Both are the same form: **alacağam** is the standard written version, **alacam** is how it sounds in conversation.

This form expresses a firm intention or an expected future action. It carries more weight than **alaram**, but it is not a binding promise; context still shapes how it lands.


## The Indefinite Future: Alaram

Every Azerbaijani child knows this word.

You ask your parent for something, a toy, a guitar, new shoes, and they say **alaram**. Technically, it means "I will buy it." In that situation, with that tone, repeated twice? It means: do not count on it.

> **Alaram, alaram.** - I'll get it, I'll get it. *(In that moment: stop asking.)*

But the implication comes from the situation, the repetition, and the tone, not from the verb itself.

The same **-ar / -ər** form also appears in genuine offers, conditional actions, and general truths:

> **Sənə kömək edərəm.** - I will help you. *(a reassuring offer)*  
> **Vaxtım olsa, gələrəm.** - If I have time, I will come. *(conditional)*  
> **İnsan səhv edər.** - People make mistakes. *(general truth)*

In each of these, the speaker is not being vague or dismissive. The form leaves the action less fixed than **alacağam**, but that flexibility is sometimes exactly what fits the moment.

**Alacağam** signals a firmer intention. The **-ar / -ər** form works for offers, conditional actions, general statements, and yes, the occasional parental non-commitment.


## Reported Information and Later Discoveries: Alıbmış

This one does not have a direct equivalent in English, which is why it catches learners off guard.

**Alıbmış** is a compound form; it combines **alıb** with the contracted form of **imiş**. That construction is what gives it its reported or inferred quality, and it is worth keeping it distinct from **oxumuşam**, which uses the **-mış** ending differently to describe experience.

> **Aldı.** - He bought it.  
> **Alıbmış.** - Apparently, he bought it. / He bought it, it seems.

**Alıbmış** adds a layer: you are signaling that this is not something you are stating as a plain fact from your own experience. It can mean you heard it from someone else, or that you figured it out later.

That said, **aldı** also appears in historical accounts and factual narratives; speakers use it even for events they never witnessed. The choice between the two is not always a strict rule about who was present. Context, register, and what you are communicating about your source all play a role.

This form also appears constantly in storytelling and folktales, where the narrator is passing on something told to them across generations.

English handles this through phrases like "apparently," "I heard that," or "it seems." Azerbaijani builds it into the verb itself.


## When You Need "Was Doing" or "Had Done"

Two more patterns help when telling a story.

For an action that was already in progress at a past moment:

> **Sən zəng edəndə işləyirdim.** - I was working when you called.

**İşləyirdim** gives the background. The work was already happening when you called.

For something completed before another past event:

> **Sən gələndə mən artıq yemişdim.** - When you arrived, I had already eaten.

**Yemişdim** places the meal before the arrival. Compare it with **yemişəm**, which describes having eaten before the present moment.


## A Quick Guide to Choosing

- Something happening now or regularly → **oxuyuram**
- Emphasizing an ongoing process → **oxumaqdayam**
- A past event you are recounting → **oxudum**
- A completed action or experience relevant to now → **oxumuşam**
- An intention or expectation held at an earlier moment → **oxuyacaqdım**
- A future plan or intention (standard spelling) → **oxuyacağam**
- An offer, conditional future action, or general statement → **oxuyaram**
- Reported information or a later discovery → **oxuyubmuş**
- An action in progress in the past → **oxuyurdum**
- An action completed before another past event → **oxumuşdum**

Time words help too. **İndi** means now. **Dünən** means yesterday. **Sabah** means tomorrow. **Artıq** means already. Learn them alongside the verb forms you use most.


## Closing Thoughts

Start with sentences you actually need to say. Tell someone what you are doing, what happened yesterday, somewhere you have been, and what you plan to do tomorrow.

Then listen for how people express the same things. Notice when they use **gəldim** and when they use **gəlmişəm**. Notice how a plan sounds with **gələcəyəm** versus **gələrəm**.

The forms become easier to choose when you know the conversations they belong in. And the more you listen, the more you start to feel when something sounds right.
